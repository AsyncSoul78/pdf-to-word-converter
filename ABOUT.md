# About the PDF to Word converter

A PDF is good for reading and bad for changing. Copy text out of one and you get broken lines, lost bold and tables that fall apart. [Toolster PDF to Word](https://toolster.net/pdf_to_word) puts the document back together instead, so the DOCX opens in Word as a normal, editable file.

## What gets rebuilt

- **Paragraphs.** Lines are joined back into paragraphs, and words split by a hyphen at the end of a line become whole again.
- **Headings.** Large text becomes Word headings.
- **Lists.** Bulleted lines become real Word lists.
- **Tables.** Tables drawn with lines turn into Word tables with the same columns and merged cells. Tables made only of aligned text, with no lines, come out as text separated by tabs.
- **Pictures** stay where they were.
- **Columns.** Two-column text is read column by column.
- **Headers and page numbers** that repeat on every page go into the Word header and footer, not into the text.

## Editable text or Exact layout

**Editable text** gives normal flowing paragraphs: the best choice when you are going to change or reuse the text.

**Exact layout** puts every line in its own box at its original position over a picture of the page. It looks like the PDF, but long edits are less comfortable.

## Scanned PDFs

Some PDFs are just pictures of pages, a scanned letter for example. Such pages are found automatically and their text is recognized in English, Russian or Spanish. The first time it happens the browser downloads the recognition data (a few megabytes) and keeps it for next time. Clean scans at 300 dpi give the best result. PDFs with real text skip this step.

## Fonts

The Word file asks for the same font as the PDF. If that font is not installed on your computer, Word shows a similar one.

## Passwords

A password protected PDF converts if you know the password: type it next to the file name.

## Privacy

The PDF is read and converted on your own device and is never uploaded, so it is fine for contracts and other private papers.
